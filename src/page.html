Using the in-game map

In game, zoom to a location, right-click and choose Mark Coordinates. Copy the chat line into A or B. Press Enter or Apply. Pasted coordinates always use game units.

Watch the order. On the map the game prints the y value above the point and the x value to its right, so reading down the screen hands you y first. Type them in that order and your point lands somewhere else entirely — x85.91, y72.59 and x72.59, y85.91 are both real places, so nothing here can warn you. Paste the chat line rather than retyping and the labels travel with it; this box reads them in either order.

Drag empty ground to pan; scroll or pinch to zoom. Drag A or B to edit, or use Set on map. A completed pair stays in place until you edit it.

Focus the map: arrow keys pan, +/− zoom, Enter selects at the crosshair, A/B edits an endpoint, N starts a new measurement, U undoes, F fits the pair, 0 fits the map, Escape cancels.

The terrain overview is about 6.4 meters per source pixel. For precise points use game coordinates; whole-meter results do not imply whole-meter image accuracy.

Calibration

One grid square is 1 km and the in-game readout is in hundreds of metres, so x72.88 / y105.33 is 7288 m east, 10533 m north. Y increases northward — grid 12 sits above grid 10. The whole terrain runs 0 to 163.84 on both axes, 16.384 km a side.

Bearings are grid north, clockwise, with mils on the 6400-per-circle scale — the same scale the gun sights use. The board's edge scale is in those same readout numbers. Markers are kept in this browser only.

The map

The board is the whole terrain square in plan view. Measure pins two points and gives you the range between them; Marker, Observer and Gun drop that thing where you click. Anything already on the board can be dragged, drag empty ground to pan, scroll to zoom. Each map keeps its own positions, markers and image, so Ozeti's targets never turn up on Bakurani.

Each map ships a plan-view render of its terrain. It covers exactly the square the board draws, so it is pinned corner to corner and needs no lining up: Fade takes it back under the grid, Remove clears it, Shipped map puts it back where it belongs. The renders are downscaled hard to keep the page quick, so they are for recognising ground, not for reading it.

To use your own instead, drop or paste a screenshot of the in-game map, then use Adjust to drag and scale it until its grid sits on the board's grid. It is stored in this browser only, and alignment by eye is exactly as accurate as you make it: the numbers come from where you click, so a sloppily placed image gives sloppy ranges. The grid and the coordinate readout are the truth, the picture is a convenience.

Observer and gun

With Gun at a separate position off, every solution is measured from you. Turn it on and the range card solves from the gun instead, with a From obs column giving your own range and bearing to the same marker — spot from one place, fire from another.

Weapon envelopes

Pick a weapon and the band column becomes a real answer instead of a distance bracket. L81 mortar reaches 52–685 m on level ground, high arc only, sight 120–950 mil. SPH-2 reaches 745–2660 m, elevation 0–1400 mil, with the low arc only available from about 1070 m out.

Spread is the grouping radius at that range, from the gun's angular dispersion — 50 MOA for the mortar, 10 MOA for the SPH-2. It is range × MOA × π / 10800, which puts the mortar at about ±10 m at its maximum and the SPH-2 at about ±7.7 m at its own.

What this does not know

Every figure here is flat-ground and two-dimensional. There is no terrain height, and altitude is the biggest error term in indirect fire: the mortar reaches roughly 735 m firing 100 m downhill and only about 625 m firing 100 m uphill. None of these numbers have been published by Bulkhead — they are community measurement, and the sources disagree with each other. Fire a ranging shell; the first shell tells the truth.
